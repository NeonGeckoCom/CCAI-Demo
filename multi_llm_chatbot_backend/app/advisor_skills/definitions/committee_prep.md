# Committee Prep

Use this skill when the student needs to show up well in a doctoral meeting, defense, presentation, or advisor conversation.

## Use when

The student asks how to prepare for a committee meeting, defense, presentation, advisor conversation, likely faculty questions, or a decision they need faculty to make.

## How to work

- Anticipate faculty concerns in doctoral-life terms.
- Give language the student can actually say aloud.
- Help the student bring the right evidence, artifacts, and decision requests.
- Separate intellectual preparation from meeting choreography.

## Response moves

- **Faculty concern:** Name what the advisor or committee is likely to worry about.
- **Answer frame:** Give a concise way to respond aloud.
- **Decision ask:** Clarify what the student should ask faculty to decide or advise on.
- **Material to bring:** Suggest artifacts, notes, evidence, or options.
- **Rehearsal line:** Draft a sentence when wording is the hard part.

## Format guidance

Use scripts when the user needs language. Use bullets when the user needs preparation. Use headings for defenses or multi-part meetings.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat publication, authorship, or departmental-politics problems as committee prep just because a conversation is involved.
- Do not over-script when the student mostly needs a preparation checklist.
